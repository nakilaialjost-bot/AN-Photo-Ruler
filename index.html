Calibrate on your own: tape a known distance from your feet to a mark on the floor (for example 3000 mm). Stand on your start point, aim the cross at the mark and tap Calibrate. The app works out your camera height.

Keep the phone at the same height for every reading, stand on level ground, and hold it upright. Typical accuracy is ±3–5% within 5 m; width uses the compass and is less accurate. Check one taped item first.

Working alone on site

  1. Stand square to the wall. Phone held level, centred on the area you want, at roughly mid-height. Fill the frame but keep corners in shot.
  2. Put a known size in the same plane. Tape the width of one door or window, or stick a 1 m length of masking tape on the wall. Everything you measure must be on that same wall face.
  3. Use a rectangle when you can't stand square. A window frame or a sheet of known size, with all 4 corners visible, lets the ruler correct the angle.
  4. Long runs: tape on your own. Hook the tape over a screw, nail or clamp at one end, or use the post spacing: measure one bay, then count bays.
  5. Check yourself. Measure one item twice from two photos. Differences above 1–2% mean the photo wasn't square: retake it.

Getting 99% accuracy

  1. Use Reference rectangle, not a line. It corrects for camera tilt and angle, so you don't need to stand straight.
  2. Make the reference big: a whole door frame or a 2 m x 1 m rectangle marked with tape, taped once. The bigger it is in the photo, the better.
  3. Measure inside or close to the reference. Reaching far beyond it adds error, and the meter will show that.
  4. Zoom in before tapping and use the magnifier. Each tap matters.
  5. Watch the badge: green means 99% or better, amber 97–99%, red below 97%: retake or use a bigger reference.

Everything you measure must be on the same flat surface as the reference. The badge covers tapping error, not a reference that was taped wrong.
